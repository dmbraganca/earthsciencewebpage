Maps, Location, and Cartographers

South Africa — Earth Processes

Location and General Area

South Africa is located on the continent of Africa, specifically in the southernmost part of the African continent. It borders the Atlantic Ocean on the west and the Indian Ocean on the east. Neighboring countries include Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and Eswatini, and it surrounds Lesotho.

Latitudinal Range

South Africa stretches approximately from 22° South latitude to 35° South latitude. Because it is entirely in the Southern Hemisphere, its seasons are opposite those in the Northern Hemisphere.

Time Periods

The region of South Africa has been inhabited for thousands of years by indigenous peoples such as the San and Khoikhoi. Major historical periods include:

  • 1652 – Dutch settlement established at Cape Town by the Dutch East India Company.
  • 1806 – Britain took permanent control of the Cape Colony.
  • 1910 – Formation of the Union of South Africa under British rule.
  • 1961 – South Africa became an independent republic.
  • 1994 – End of apartheid and establishment of modern democratic South Africa under Nelson Mandela.

Maps and Cartography

Maps of South Africa can be found in various formats, including physical maps, political maps, and thematic maps that highlight specific features such as climate zones, vegetation, or population density. The South African government and various organizations provide detailed maps for educational and navigational purposes.

Famous Cartographers and Their Influence

Willem Janszoon Blaeu

Willem Janszoon Blaeu was a famous Dutch cartographer whose maps influenced early European understanding of southern Africa during the 1600s.

Major accomplishments:

  • Created highly accurate world maps and atlases.
  • Improved navigation and coastal mapping for European explorers.
  • Helped map trade routes around the Cape of Good Hope.

Jacobus Hondius

Jodocus Hondius also influenced mapping of South Africa during the Age of Exploration.

Major accomplishments:

  • Published detailed atlases used by sailors and explorers.
  • Improved maps of Africa based on Portuguese and Dutch voyages.
  • Helped spread geographic knowledge across Europe.

South African Mapping Development

Modern South African cartography later developed through organizations such as the Chief Directorate: National Geo-spatial Information, which produces official topographic and geographic maps for the country today.
