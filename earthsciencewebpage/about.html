An Interesting Feature of Iceland's Physical Geography

One of the most striking things about Iceland's physical geography is that it sits directly on top of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge — the seam where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates slowly pull apart. Most of this ridge lies two miles beneath the Atlantic Ocean, but in Iceland the ridge rises above sea level, creating an island that is quite literally being split in two at a rate of about two centimeters per year. The result is a landscape of active volcanoes, geysers, hot springs, and lava fields sitting right beside massive glaciers. Walking across parts of Iceland is as close as you can get to walking on a brand-new planet.
