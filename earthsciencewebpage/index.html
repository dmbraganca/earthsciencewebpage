South Africa

Earth Science • PS 120 • Ensign College

South Africa is at the southern tip of Africa, where the Atlantic and Indian Oceans meet. It has a complicated history, a huge amount of geographic variety, and one of the most diverse populations anywhere. This site looks at its physical geography from an earth science perspective.

Historical Background

The San and Khoikhoi peoples lived in southern Africa long before Europeans arrived. The Dutch East India Company set up a station at Cape Town in 1652, and the British took over in 1806. The country became the Union of South Africa in 1910. From 1948 to 1994, apartheid enforced racial segregation under white minority rule. It ended when Nelson Mandela won the first fully democratic election in 1994 — a pretty massive turning point in history.

Culture

South Africa has 11 official languages, which says a lot about how diverse the country is. Zulu, Xhosa, Afrikaans, and English are the main ones. It's often called the Rainbow Nation. Ubuntu — loosely "I am because we are" — is a big part of how people think about community here. South Africa has some of the most delicious food in the world. Braai (WAY better than barbecue) cuts across all groups and is pretty much a national tradition. Rugby and cricket are the big sports, and South Africa hosted the FIFA World Cup in 2010.

Society

About 62 million people live across nine provinces. Johannesburg, Cape Town, and Durban are the biggest cities. Inequality is a serious ongoing issue — South Africa is one of the most unequal countries in the world by income. Unemployment sits above 30% and hasn't budged much. Load shedding — rolling blackouts from the struggling state power company Eskom — has become a normal part of daily life for most people.

Economy

South Africa has the biggest economy on the continent. Mining is still a huge part of it — gold, platinum, and coal are the top exports. Manufacturing, agriculture, and tourism all matter too. It's a member of BRICS and the G20. Growth has been slow lately, mostly because of the energy crisis and the unemployment problem. Tourism is one of the brighter spots, with places like Cape Town, Kruger National Park, and the Drakensberg drawing a lot of visitors.
